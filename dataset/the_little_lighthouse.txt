The Little Lighthouse

Once there was a small lighthouse on a rocky island. It was not tall, but it was brave. Every night, it shone its light across the dark sea.

One evening, a fierce storm came. The wind howled, and the waves rose high. The lighthouse keeper, an old man named Tom, watched the sea with worried eyes. His little boat was tied to the dock, but the rope was old.

A young girl named Mia lived with her grandfather Tom. She saw the rope and said, "Grandfather, the rope is weak. The boat may float away."

Tom nodded. "We must fix it, but the storm is too strong."

Mia looked at the lighthouse. "The light must keep shining," she said. "Ships need it."

Tom tried to climb the stairs, but his leg hurt. Mia took the lamp and climbed instead. The stairs were steep, and the wind shook the tower. But she did not stop.

At the top, she lit the lamp. The light cut through the rain. Far away, a small fishing boat saw it and turned toward the safe harbor.

In the morning, the storm was gone. The fisherman came to the island. "Your light saved us," he said.

Mia smiled. She was small, but she had done a big thing. And the little lighthouse stood proud, shining for every ship that passed.
